The two formulas

Adding GST: price × 1.1. The GST is price × 0.1.

Finding GST inside a price: price ÷ 11. The ex-GST price is price ÷ 1.1.

The second one trips people up. Because GST is 10% of the ex-GST price, it is one-eleventh of the GST-inclusive price, not 10% of it. Taking 10% off a $110 bill gives $99, which is wrong: the real ex-GST price is $100 and the GST is $10.

When GST applies

GST is a 10% tax on most goods and services sold in Australia. Businesses must register for GST once their GST turnover reaches $75,000 a year ($150,000 for non-profits), and every taxi and rideshare driver must register regardless of turnover. A business that is not registered cannot charge GST, so a quote "plus GST" from an unregistered supplier is a red flag: ask for their ABN and check it on the ABN Lookup site.

Some things are GST-free, including most basic food (bread, milk, fresh fruit and vegetables), many health and medical services, most education courses and childcare. Prepared food, snacks and drinks other than plain milk and water generally do include GST, which is why a supermarket receipt often mixes the two.

Quick tips

  • Advertised prices to consumers in Australia must include GST, so the shelf price is the price you pay.
  • Business-to-business quotes are often written "ex GST". Add 10% before comparing them with a consumer price.
  • If you are GST-registered, the GST on your business purchases can usually be claimed back as a GST credit on your BAS, so the real cost to the business is the ex-GST amount.
