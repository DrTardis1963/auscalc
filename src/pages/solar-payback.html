How the estimate works

  1. Yearly output = system kW × average daily yield for your city × 365.
  2. The share you use yourself saves you your full electricity rate.
  3. The rest is exported and earns the feed-in tariff.
  4. Payback = system cost ÷ yearly saving.

Yearly averages for well-placed, unshaded north-facing panels, as commonly used by installers. East/west roofs produce roughly 10-15% less; shade can cut output a lot more.

Worked example: 6.6 kW in Brisbane

6.6 kW × 4.2 × 365 = about 10,118 kWh a year. Using 40% of it at home (4,047 kWh) avoids buying power at 32c, saving $1,295. Exporting the other 6,071 kWh at 5c earns $304. Total saving: about $1,599 a year, so a $5,500 system pays for itself in roughly 3½ years.

Self-use matters more than the feed-in tariff

Every kWh you use yourself is worth your full electricity rate, often six times what an export earns. Moving loads into the sunny part of the day lifts your savings without spending anything: run the dishwasher, washing machine, pool pump and hot water timer between about 10am and 3pm, and pre-cool or pre-heat the house with the air conditioner in the early afternoon. A battery does the same thing automatically, but at current prices it usually lengthens the payback period of the whole system.

Before you sign a quote

  • The price on most quotes already has the federal small-scale (STC) rebate taken off. That rebate steps down each year until it ends in 2030, so the same system gets slightly dearer each January.
  • Use an installer accredited by Solar Accreditation Australia, and ask for the panel and inverter brands and warranties in writing.
  • Inverters often need replacing after about 10-12 years. Budget for one replacement over the life of the panels.
