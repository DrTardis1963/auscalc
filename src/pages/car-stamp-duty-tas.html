All rates are per $100 or part of $100. A passenger vehicle carries up to 9 adults, including off-road vehicles without a ute body. Utes, panel vans, motorcycles and vehicles seating more than 9 are "other" vehicles.

The $35,000 to $40,000 step

Tasmania moves from 3% to 4% by charging 11% on the slice between $35,000 and $40,000. By $40,000 the duty has caught up with 4% of the whole value, and from there it is a flat 4%. So each extra $1,000 on a car in that range costs $110 in duty, against $30 below it and $40 above.

A $50,000 ute pays 500 × $3 = $1,500, and a $40,000 car bought new with a fleet discount pays 400 × $3.50 = $1,400.

What value is the duty charged on?

  • New car: the purchase price, including the value of any trade-in, accessories and after-market treatments fitted before delivery, dealer delivery, GST and luxury car tax.
  • Used car: the greatest of the price paid (including trade-ins), the market value when you acquired it, and the market value when you apply to register.
  • Bought at auction: the total paid to the auction house, including the buyer's premium, card fees and delivery.

Electric and hybrid cars

Tasmania exempted new and second-hand electric and hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles registered between 1 July 2021 and 30 June 2023, and new ones contracted before 25 May 2023 and registered by 31 December 2023. That has ended: an EV registered now pays the normal rates. Hybrids pay the normal rates too.

What this leaves out

  • Registration fees and the motor accident insurance premium. The certificate for that premium carries its own $20 duty.
  • Exemptions, exemption certificates and the separate rules for motor vehicle dealers and traders.
  • Heavy vehicles over 4.5 tonnes: $1 per $100, minimum $20.
  • Deadlines: the seller lodges a disposal notice within 7 days, and the buyer applies to transfer within 14 days.
