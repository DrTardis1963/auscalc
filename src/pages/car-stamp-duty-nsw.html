NSW motor vehicle duty rates

Vehicle valuePassenger vehicleOther vehicles
Up to $44,999$3 per $100$3 per $100
$45,000 or more$1,350 plus $5 per $100 over $45,000$3 per $100

A passenger vehicle is a sedan, wagon, coupe, convertible, 4WD with seats and windows, people mover, small bus or motor home. The 5% tier does not apply to motorcycles, buses for more than nine people, hearses or disability vehicles, which pay 3% on the whole value.

Only the part above $45,000 is charged at 5%, so the duty rises smoothly: there is no jump at the threshold. This calculator counts a part-$100 as a whole $100, the usual rule for this duty; Service NSW's own figure is the one you pay.

Worked example: a $50,000 new SUV

Duty on the first $45,000$1,350
$5,000 above $45,000 = 50 lots of $100 × $5$250
Stamp duty$1,600

That is 3.2% of the price. A $30,000 used hatchback costs 300 × $3 = $900, and a $60,000 dual-motor EV costs $1,350 + 150 × $5 = $2,100. A $60,000 vehicle that is not a passenger vehicle pays the flat 3%: $1,800.

Revenue NSW's passenger list includes "four-wheel drive vehicle, with seats and windows", which can take in a 4WD dual-cab ute. If you are not sure which side of the line your ute falls on, ask Service NSW before you rely on the lower figure.

What value is the duty charged on?

  • New car: the amount you paid, including GST. A factory rebate paid to you after the sale does not lower it, but an extended warranty bought on top is not included.
  • Used car: the sale price or the market value, whichever is higher. Both the seller and buyer write the market value on the transfer form. If you are unsure of it, ask a dealer or your insurer or look up the make, model and year.
  • Undervaluing costs more. Revenue NSW checks declared values. If it finds the value was understated it charges the missing duty, daily interest and penalty tax of up to 90% of the shortfall.

Electric and hybrid cars in NSW

NSW exempted battery electric and hydrogen fuel cell cars worth up to $78,000 from duty between 1 September 2021 and 31 December 2023. That exemption has ended: an EV registered now pays the same rates as a petrol car. Buyers who signed a contract or paid a deposit on an eligible EV before 1 January 2024 can still apply for a refund. Hybrids never had a concession.

What this leaves out

  • Registration, CTP (green slip) and the transfer fee, which are separate charges.
  • Exemptions, such as transfers between spouses, changing the name on a registration, and some disability vehicles, charities and caravans. Revenue NSW's exemptions page lists the evidence each needs.
  • Duty relief for a replacement car when yours was destroyed in a declared natural disaster.
  • Heavy vehicles and dealer-only rules.
