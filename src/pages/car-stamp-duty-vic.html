Two things make Victoria different from most states:

  • One rate on the whole value. The rate for your band applies to every dollar, not just the part above the threshold. Crossing $80,809 by a little lifts the duty on the entire car.
  • Per $200, rounded up. The value is rounded up to the next $200 before the rate is applied, so $45,001 is charged as $45,200.

Non-passenger vehicles are motorcycles, vehicles built for more than eight adults, and vehicles mainly designed to carry goods, including utes and dual-cab utes.

The jump from $80,809 to $81,000 is the one to watch: $191 more on the price adds $810 of duty. If you are negotiating near a band edge, getting the price under it saves more than the discount itself. The green-car rate only saves money on cars above $80,809; below that the green and normal rates are the same.

What value is the duty charged on?

  • The higher of the purchase price (including GST and any luxury car tax) and the market value when you register or transfer.
  • For a used car the value is worked out when you lodge the transfer with VicRoads, not when you bought it.
  • Repairs or improvements made before registration, such as work to pass a roadworthy, are added to the value.
  • VicRoads does not check the value you declare, but the SRO compares it with an industry valuation guide and can send a reassessment.

Electric, hybrid and green cars

A green car is a passenger car with combined tailpipe CO2 emissions of 120 grams per kilometre or less. Every fully electric car qualifies, and so do many hybrids and plug-in hybrids; look up the "combined" figure in the federal Green Vehicle Guide or the manufacturer's specifications. The green rate stays at $8.40 per $200 at every price, which is worth $900 on a $90,000 car and more above $100,000.

What this leaves out

  • Registration, the TAC charge and transfer fees, which VicRoads charges separately.
  • Exemptions and refunds, such as transfers between spouses or domestic partners and some vehicles for veterans, primary producers and people with a disability.
  • Licensed motor car trader rules, change-of-use duty and heavy trailers.
  • Dealer drive-away prices, which the SRO treats with their own worked examples.
