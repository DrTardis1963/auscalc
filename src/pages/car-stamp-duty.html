A $50,000 car costs more than twice as much in duty in Western Australia as in the Northern Territory, and a buyer in Darwin pays nothing on a $50,000 EV until June 2027.

Why the states give different answers

  • Step or slab. NSW, South Australia and the ACT charge their higher rate only on the part of the price above a threshold, so the duty climbs smoothly. Victoria, Queensland, Western Australia and part of Tasmania's scale apply one rate to the whole price, so crossing a threshold by a dollar can add hundreds.
  • Per $100 or part. Most states count every started $100 (Victoria: every started $200) as a whole one. A $30,050 car is taxed as if it cost $30,100.
  • What counts as the value. For a used car most states use the price you paid or the market value, whichever is higher, so a cheap sale to a friend does not cut the duty. For a new car, Queensland and Western Australia use the manufacturer's list price, not your discounted deal.
  • Engine and emissions. Queensland charges by cylinders and gives hybrids and EVs a lower rate. The ACT charges by CO2 emissions per kilometre. Victoria has one green-car rate. The NT has an EV concession until 30 June 2027. NSW and Tasmania no longer give EVs a break.
  • Utes and vans. Most states treat goods vehicles as commercial and charge less on dearer ones: no 5% tier in NSW, 3% rather than 4% in South Australia, a flat rate in Tasmania, and a cheaper new-vehicle rate in Victoria.

What this leaves out

  • Registration, CTP insurance and transfer fees. These are separate charges, paid at the same time. Dealer "drive-away" prices usually include them along with the duty.
  • Luxury car tax. That is a federal tax already inside the price of an expensive new car. It is not stamp duty, but most states charge duty on a price that includes it.
  • Exemptions. Transfers between spouses, deceased estates, some disability and veteran vehicles and moving a car you already own from another state are often exempt. Each state page lists where to check.
  • Heavy vehicles, motorcycles, caravans and trailers. These have their own rates and are not covered here.
