Light vehicles are cars, 4WDs, utes, vans and motorbikes up to 4.5 tonnes gross vehicle mass. Heavier vehicles pay the "up to $100,000" rate at any price. Special vehicles such as forklifts, tractors and graders pay a flat $25.

How the duty is worked out, step by step

  1. Find the dutiable value (next section). For a new car that is the list price plus options, not the price you negotiated.
  2. Count the $100s, rounding up. Duty is charged "per $100 or part of $100", so $45,050 counts as 451 lots of $100, not 450.
  3. Pick the rate from the engine type and whether the value is over $100,000.
  4. Multiply. 451 × $3 = $1,353 for a $45,050 four-cylinder car.

The rate applies to the whole value. Once a car is worth more than $100,000, the higher rate covers every dollar, not only the part above $100,000. Queensland's own estimator agrees: a $110,050 four-cylinder car is charged 1,101 × $5 = $5,505.

Dutiable value: what the duty is charged on

  • New car: the list price, meaning the manufacturer's recommended retail price including GST and luxury car tax, plus any optional equipment. A discount you negotiate does not lower it.
  • Used car, or a new car with no list price: the higher of the total you paid and the car's market value. The total paid counts any deposit, trade-in allowance, dealer delivery, warranties and options.
  • Repaired write-offs and imported cars: the market value when the car is registered, so repairs done before registration raise the duty.
  • Modified for a person with a disability: the cost of the modifications comes off the value.

The $100,000 line

Because the higher rate covers the whole price, crossing $100,000 by a single dollar roughly doubles the duty on a hybrid or EV and lifts it by two thirds on a four-cylinder car:

If a car lists just over $100,000, dropping a dealer-fitted option can bring the dutiable value under the line and save about $2,000. A discount on the price will not, because a new car's duty is based on its list price.

Hybrids and electric cars

Queensland is one of the few states where a hybrid pays less duty than a petrol car. Any hybrid, whatever its cylinder count and including plug-in hybrids, and any fully electric car pays $2 per $100 up to $100,000 and $4 above. A $45,000 hybrid pays $900, which is $450 less than a four-cylinder petrol car at the same price.

When and how you pay

  • New car from a dealer: the dealer normally registers the car for you, and the duty, rego and CTP are part of the drive-away price.
  • Private sale: the buyer transfers the registration and pays the duty. You must do it within 14 days of buying or receiving the car, and the seller must give you a current safety certificate (unless the vehicle is exempt).
  • Duty on a private sale is worked out on the higher of the sale price (including GST if any) and the market value. Selling to a stranger for a token price does not cut the duty.
  • Disagree with the assessment? You can lodge an objection within 60 days of paying.

Moving to Queensland with your car

A car registered in another state that will be kept at a Queensland address must be registered in Queensland within 14 days, and you can be fined if it is not. You do not pay duty again if it was paid when the car was registered interstate. You will need to show the interstate duty was paid.

Who does not pay duty

You may be able to claim an exemption if you:

  • receive the car as a gift from a family member: a spouse (including de facto), parent, grandparent, child, stepchild or grandchild. Gifts between siblings, aunts, uncles or in-laws do not count.
  • receive the car from a deceased estate
  • are re-registering a car you or a relative previously registered in Queensland, or one already registered interstate with duty paid
  • are an ex-service person, have lost the use of either leg, or need a motorised wheelchair
  • are a dealer, a charity, a primary producer, part of a small business restructure, or transferring the car in a matrimonial matter

There is no duty discount for seniors or pensioner card holders, but they pay less registration (see the QLD rego calculator). Each exemption has conditions: check the Queensland Government's exemptions page before you rely on one.

Common questions

How much is stamp duty on a $50,000 car in Queensland?

$1,500 for a petrol or diesel car with up to 4 cylinders (500 × $3), $1,750 for 5 or 6 cylinders, $2,000 for 7 or more, and $1,000 for a hybrid or electric car.

Do I pay stamp duty on a used car in Queensland?

Yes. Every time registration moves to a new owner, the buyer pays duty on the higher of the price paid and the market value, unless an exemption applies.

Is the duty charged on the price I negotiated?

Not for a new car with a list price: the duty is on the list price plus options. For a used car, it is on what you paid or the market value, whichever is higher.

Are electric cars exempt from stamp duty in Queensland?

No, but they pay the lowest rate: $2 per $100 up to $100,000 and $4 per $100 above.

Does the higher rate over $100,000 apply only to the amount above $100,000?

No. It applies to the whole value, which is why a $100,100 EV pays $4,004 while a $100,000 EV pays $2,000.

Which calculator does Queensland use: TMR, QRO or OSR?

The official duty estimator is on qld.gov.au, and duty is administered by the Queensland Revenue Office (many people still search for it as "OSR"). This page uses the same rates and rounding, and also shows the working.

Do I pay duty on a caravan, trailer or boat?

No. Vehicle registration duty does not apply to caravans, trailers or boats.

What this leaves out

  • Contracts signed before 1 July 2018, which use older rates.
  • Business and fleet arrangements, and duty credits when a business restructures.
  • Dealer charges such as delivery and pre-delivery fees: see the drive-away price calculator.
  • Exact CTP from your insurer; the range above covers all Class 1 insurers.
