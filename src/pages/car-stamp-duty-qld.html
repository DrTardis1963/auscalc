Queensland car stamp duty calculator

Vehicle registration duty, paid to the Department of Transport and Main Roads when you register or transfer a car in Queensland. The rate depends on the engine.

For light vehicles: cars, 4WDs, utes and motorbikes up to 4.5 tonnes gross vehicle mass.

Each rate is per $100 or part of $100, and it applies to the whole value. Once a car is worth more than $100,000 the higher rate covers every dollar, not only the part above $100,000. Queensland's own estimator agrees: a $110,050 four-cylinder car is charged 1,101 × $5 = $5,505.

The last two rows show the $100,000 cliff: $100 more on the list price doubles the duty, adding $2,004. Choosing a hybrid over the same-priced four-cylinder saves a third of the duty, $450 on a $45,000 car.

What value is the duty charged on?

  • New car: the list price, meaning the manufacturer's recommended retail price including GST and luxury car tax, plus whatever you paid for optional equipment. A discount you negotiate does not lower it. Buy a car listed at $60,000 for $56,000 and you still pay duty on $60,000.
  • Used car, or a new car with no list price: the higher of the total you paid (counting any deposit, trade-in allowance, dealer delivery, warranties and options) and the market value. A cheap sale between family members is charged on market value.
  • Repaired or imported cars: the market value when registered, so repairs made before registration raise the duty.
  • Modified for a person with a disability: the cost of the modification is taken off the value.

Hybrids and electric cars

Queensland is one of the few states where a hybrid pays less duty than a petrol car. Any hybrid, whatever its cylinder count, and any fully electric car pays the 2% rate up to $100,000 and 4% above. That makes a $45,000 hybrid $450 cheaper to register than a four-cylinder petrol car at the same price.

What this leaves out

  • Registration fees and CTP insurance, which are separate.
  • Exemptions for family transfers, dealers, businesses and charities, and the motorised-wheelchair exemption.
  • Heavy vehicles over 4.5 tonnes, which pay the lower rate at every value, and conditionally registered machines such as forklifts and tractors ($25).
  • Contracts signed before 1 July 2018, which use the older rates.
  • Caravans, trailers and boats, which pay no vehicle registration duty.
