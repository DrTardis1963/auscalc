How the 2026-27 tax is worked out

From 1 July 2026 the tax rate on income between $18,201 and $45,000 dropped from 16% to 15%. The other brackets are unchanged. For an Australian resident who claims the tax-free threshold:

Taxable incomeTax on this income
$0 – $18,200Nil
$18,201 – $45,00015c for each $1 over $18,200
$45,001 – $135,000$4,020 plus 30c for each $1 over $45,000
$135,001 – $190,000$31,020 plus 37c for each $1 over $135,000
$190,001 and over$51,370 plus 45c for each $1 over $190,000

Two more things change the final number:

  • Low income tax offset (LITO). Up to $700 off your tax if you earn $37,500 or less. It shrinks by 5c for each dollar above $37,500, then by 1.5c per dollar above $45,000, and is gone at $66,667. It can cut your tax to zero but is never paid out as cash.
  • Medicare levy. 2% of taxable income. Low earners pay less: nothing at or below $28,011, then 10c for each dollar above that until the full 2% applies (the thresholds shown are the latest the ATO has published, for 2025-26; they usually rise a little each year).

Worked example: $80,000 a year

  1. Income tax: $4,020 + 30% × ($80,000 − $45,000) = $4,020 + $10,500 = $14,520.
  2. LITO: none, because $80,000 is above $66,667.
  3. Medicare levy: 2% × $80,000 = $1,600.
  4. Take-home: $80,000 − $14,520 − $1,600 = $63,880 a year, or about $2,456.92 a fortnight.

Your employer also pays 12% super on top: $9,600 a year that goes into your super fund, not your bank account.

Worked example: $35,000 a year

This one shows both low-income breaks at work. Income tax is 15% × $16,800 = $2,520. The full $700 LITO brings that to $1,820. The Medicare levy is in its phase-in range, so it is 10% × ($35,000 − $28,011) = $698.90 instead of the full $700. Total tax $2,518.90, take-home $32,481.10. That is an average tax rate of about 7.2%.

Marginal rate vs average rate

Your average rate is total tax divided by income. Your marginal rate is what you keep from your next dollar, which matters for overtime, a pay rise or a side job. At $80,000 the marginal rate is 32% (30% tax + 2% Medicare): a $1,000 bonus leaves $680 in your pocket. Between $37,500 and $45,000 the shrinking LITO adds an extra 5c per dollar, so earners there lose more of each extra dollar than the 15% headline rate suggests.

What this calculator leaves out

  • HELP/HECS study loan repayments.
  • The Medicare levy surcharge (for higher earners without private hospital cover).
  • Deductions, salary sacrifice, other tax offsets and family-income Medicare reductions.
  • Non-residents and working holiday makers, who pay different rates.

Your employer's PAYG withholding each payday can differ by a few dollars from the yearly figure; the difference comes back (or is owed) when you lodge your tax return.
