The three parts of your rego

  • Registration fee: set by the number of cylinders. It goes up each 1 July; for 2026–27 the increase was 3.4%.
  • Traffic improvement fee: $67.25 for every private car, whatever its size.
  • CTP insurance: covers people injured in a crash involving your car. You choose the insurer, and the premium for a private car (Class 1) runs from $411.80 to $427.80 for 12 months. That $16 spread is the most you can save by switching.

Concession rates

Holders of an eligible concession or seniors card get about half off the registration fee. The traffic improvement fee and CTP do not change.

When you buy a used car, the seller's registration normally stays with the car until it expires: you pay the transfer fee and duty, then the full renewal when it falls due. You must transfer the registration within 14 days of buying the car.

Paying for less than 12 months

You can renew for a shorter period. A surcharge applies, covering the extra notices and processing, so 12 months is the cheapest way to pay. The Queensland Government's quote tool gives exact shorter-term amounts.

What this leaves out

  • Business-use, primary producer and heavy vehicles, which have their own fees.
  • Motorbikes, caravans and trailers.
  • Late renewal fees and shorter-term surcharges.
  • Your insurer's exact CTP premium, which you can compare on the Motor Accident Insurance Commission's website.
