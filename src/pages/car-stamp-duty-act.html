ACT car stamp duty calculator

Motor vehicle duty in the ACT depends on the car's CO2 emissions, whether it is new or used, and the date you register it. Paid at Access Canberra within 14 days of buying the car.

Passenger and light commercial vehicles under 4.5 tonnes, including utes and vans.

How ACT duty is worked out

The car's category sets two rates, one for value under $45,000 and one for the slice from $45,000 to $80,000. Above $80,000 every category pays $8 per $100 on the extra. Each rate is per $100 or part of $100.

Under $45,000: rate A × value. $45,000 to $80,000: (rate A × $45,000) + rate B × the amount over $45,000. Over $80,000: the duty at $80,000 + $8 per $100 over $80,000.

Used vehicles

A used car pays the AAA rates if it is a zero-emission vehicle. Every other used car, whatever its category, pays the category C rates for its period: $3.17 and $5.62 per $100 up to 31 January 2027, and $3.72 and $6.44 from 1 February 2027.

New or used, and finding the category

  • New means never registered in Australia or overseas, including a demonstrator sold within a year of its first ACT registration. Used means registered before, including demonstrators registered outside the ACT.
  • Find the car's combined CO2 figure in the federal Green Vehicle Guide (the column labelled "Comb") or from the manufacturer. If no figure can be found, the car is non-rated and pays category C rates.
  • The ACT rates on emissions, not on engine type. Most hybrids fall in A and most plug-in hybrids in AA, but a large hybrid can land in B.

What this leaves out

  • Registration, the compulsory third-party (MAI) premium and other Access Canberra fees.
  • Exempt vehicles, such as non-motorised caravans and camper trailers, and refunds.
  • Bringing a car from another state: no duty if it stays in the same name, duty was paid in the other state and the old registration has not expired.
  • Motorcycles, trucks, buses and motorhomes, which have their own rates.
