EV vs petrol running cost

Compare what you would spend a year on petrol with what an electric car costs to charge.

How it compares

Petrol cost per 100 km = L/100km × $ per litre. EV cost per 100 km = kWh/100km × c/kWh ÷ 100.

Worked example: 12,000 km a year

OptionPer 100 kmPer year
Petrol, 7.5 L/100km at $1.95$14.63$1,755
EV, 16 kWh/100km, home at 30c$4.80$576
EV, home off-peak at 15c$2.40$288
EV, fast charger at 60c$9.60$1,152

Charged at home the EV saves about $1,180-$1,470 a year in this example. Relying entirely on fast chargers still saves around $600, but most of the advantage disappears, which is why home charging matters so much to the EV maths.

What the yearly fuel number leaves out

  • Servicing. EVs have no oil, spark plugs or exhaust, so scheduled servicing is usually cheaper, though tyres can wear faster because EVs are heavier.
  • Purchase price and resale. The upfront price gap is usually much larger than one year of fuel savings. Divide the price difference by the yearly saving to see how many years it takes to break even.
  • Charging losses. Around 10% of the energy from the wall is lost while charging, more on a slow 10 A powerpoint. Add about 10% to the EV's kWh/100km if your figure comes from the car's dashboard.
  • Registration, insurance and any road-user charges, which vary by state and can change.

How many kilometres do Australians drive?

The ABS Survey of Motor Vehicle Use has put the average passenger car at somewhere between about 11,000 and 13,000 km a year over recent surveys. Use your own odometer: take this year's reading, subtract last year's service record, and you have your real figure. The more you drive, the faster an EV pays back.
